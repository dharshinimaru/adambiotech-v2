01 · THE PROBLEM

The lab has not been rebuilt since before the internet.

Most scientific instruments were designed before commodity sensors, open hardware, modern computer vision, edge compute, and AI existed. The entire installed base of research tools predates the components that would make them cheap and intelligent.

The result: a grad student who wants to run 24 parallel experiments has to pay hundreds of thousands of dollars for a commercial system — or cobble one together manually, with no way to monitor or automate any of it.

That gap is the opening. A whole generation of lab equipment is ready to be rebuilt.

If scientific tools become cheap enough to be abundant, connected enough to work together, and intelligent enough to operate themselves — the laboratory becomes programmable.
02 · OUR APPROACH

Open the hardware.
Close the intelligence.

We follow one rule across every instrument we build: open what is a fixed, one-time artifact. Close what compounds with use.

The mechanical designs, schematics, CAD files, and build instructions are free — forever. Once those files exist, sharing them costs nothing and gets the instrument into more hands.

The cloud orchestration, calibration models, predictive software, and fleet intelligence are closed — because they get smarter with every instrument-hour from every connected unit.

In practice: the instrument works on its own the moment you build or buy it. Connect it to us, and it becomes trustworthy, predictive, and part of something larger.

OPEN SOURCE

Free to build, forever

Mechanical designs, CAD, schematics, connector specs, bill of materials, and firmware. Once the file is made, giving it away costs nothing — and gets our tools into more hands.

CLOSED SOURCE

Where the value compounds

Cloud orchestration, calibration models, failure and anomaly detection, fleet telemetry, predictive maintenance, and the operating layer that coordinates instruments together.

03 · FIRST PRODUCT

A low-cost, parallelizable bioreactor.

We are starting with the instrument at the center of biological research: the bioreactor. Running continuously for days to weeks, already sensing and already actuating — the fastest way to prove the model and start building the intelligence layer.

Commercial parallel bioreactor systems routinely cost tens of thousands of dollars per setup. Labs that need to run 24 conditions in parallel have no affordable path. We are changing that.

Build it yourself for free using our open design files. Or buy it from us, ready to run. Either way, the reactor senses, logs, and shows you a live curve. Connect it to us, and it tells you when a run is failing before you lose a week of work.

04 · BUSINESS MODEL

We charge for intelligence, not instruments.

We do not make money from selling vessels, reagents, or service contracts. Every incumbent in this space does — and it traps them. Their whole business depends on locking labs into their ecosystem, so they can never be the neutral layer that connects everything together.

We make money from intelligence and orchestration. One flat monthly fee per instrument. No tiers. No usage charges. No surprises.

This means we are genuinely indifferent to whether you build or buy the hardware. Both choices make us stronger — every connected unit, whatever its origin, feeds the same intelligence layer.

05 · THE ROADMAP

Each stage proves
the next one.

We are not building a feature roadmap. We are building a sequence of proofs — each one produces the data and evidence that justifies the next stage.

Stage 1DETECTION

Make one tool radically more accessible

Ship the bioreactor. Collect instrument-level telemetry: growth anomalies, contamination signatures, hardware faults. Prove the instrument can tell you a run is failing while there is still time to act.

Stage 2RELIABILITY

Build the intelligence layer with a real fleet

Tubing fatigue prediction, optical drift detection, calibration decay models. A cross-fleet dataset that makes your OD data trustworthy and provable. This is the moat. It cannot be bought; it has to be accumulated.

Stage 3INTERVENTION

Close the loop: detect, act, observe

State detected, action approved, outcome recorded. The bioreactor already senses and already pumps, so this starts with instrument number one. Every turbidostat run generates verified cause-and-effect data automatically.

Stage 4OPERATING LAYER

Launch more instruments. Connect everything.

Expand to complementary tools — starting with an automated sampler, then beyond. AdamOS becomes the dashboard where scientists define workflows across all their instruments: Image these wells every hour, detect abnormal growth, dispense treatment when they reach the target state.

Stage 5AUTONOMOUS LAB

The lab runs itself.

Closed-loop experimentation. The system proposes the next condition based on the last result, runs it, interprets the outcome, and within approved limits executes. A scientist specifies a workflow. The lab does the rest.

06 · WHY WE WIN

Every incumbent is trapped by their own business model.

Thermo, Sartorius, and Eppendorf cannot open anything — their entire revenue comes from consumables, vessels, and service contracts. Opening the hardware would cannibalize the core.

Opentrons proved the pull of openness was real — their 2015 open launch generated genuine community enthusiasm. Then they quietly closed the hardware as they scaled. The instinct was right. The rule was missing.

Strateos tried to sell the full orchestration vision before proving a single instrument mattered to anyone. It nearly broke the company.

The rule each was missing: open what is a fixed one-time artifact, close what compounds with use. Our hardware can be free without threatening the business — and none of them can copy that without breaking something they already depend on.
